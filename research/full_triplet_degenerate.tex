\subsection{Exact classification of the degenerate slow boundary}
\label{sec:fulltripletdegenerate}
The full triplet premise is especially restrictive at a repeated slow
rate. In fact this boundary admits an explicit description, and its
absence of anomalous thermal ordering holds at every time for convex
divergences.

\begin{theorem}[The repeated-slow boundary is reset plus one adjacent pair]
\label{thm:tripletdegenerate}
Let $M$ be an irreducible reversible four-state generator with distinct
energy levels, positive bath temperature, and all four original-rate
triplet failures, equivalently $F_{ijk},G_{ijk}\leq0$.
Write $q=\pi(\beta_b)$, $D=\operatorname{diag}(q)$ and
$R=q\mathbf1^\top-I$. If
$0>\lambda_2=\lambda_3>\lambda_4$, then, for exactly one
$k\in\{1,2,3\}$,
\[
M=\rho R+\delta J^{(k)},\qquad \rho>0,\quad\delta>0,
\]
where $J^{(k)}$ is supported on the adjacent pair $(k,k+1)$,
\[
J^{(k)}_{k,k+1}=\frac{q_k}{q_k+q_{k+1}},\qquad
J^{(k)}_{k+1,k}=\frac{q_{k+1}}{q_k+q_{k+1}},
\]
and its diagonal is set by zero column sums. Conversely, every such
matrix obeys the full triplet premise and has spectrum
$0,-\rho,-\rho,-\rho-\delta$.
If all three nonzero eigenvalues coincide, then $M=\rho R$.

Every generator in these two degenerate classes preserves stochastic
order. Consequently, for cooling or heating Gibbs preparations, the
farther preparation has at least as large a convex divergence from the
bath at every $t\geq0$. This statement holds for every convex function
finite on positive arguments; it is strict at finite times for strictly
convex functions and distinct preparations. In particular there is no
ordinary or strong asymptotic effect in KL or weighted Euclidean distance.
No scalar choice within the repeated eigenspace is used in this conclusion.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Use normalized weights $q_1>q_2>q_3>q_4>0$.
Set $\rho=-\lambda_2$ and $\delta=\lambda_2-\lambda_4$.
The spectral theorem for the reversible generator gives
\[
M=\rho R-\frac{\delta}{N}Dzz^\top,
\qquad q^\top z=0,\qquad N=z^\top Dz>0,
\]
where $z$ is a real fast left eigenvector. In particular
$a_{ij}=\rho q_i-\delta q_i z_i z_j/N$ for $i\ne j$.
Substitution into the original triplet tests gives the exact identities
\begin{align*}
G_{ijk}&=-\frac{\delta q_j}{N}z_k(z_i-z_j),\\
F_{ijk}&=\frac{\delta}{N}\left\{
(q_i-q_j)z_i z_j-z_k\left[q_i z_i-q_j z_j
 +\frac{q_k}{2}(z_i-z_j)\right]\right\}.
\end{align*}
Thus $G\leq0$ means $z_k(z_i-z_j)\geq0$.
When $z_i=z_j=a$, the second identity reduces to
$F_{ijk}=\delta(q_i-q_j)a(a-z_k)/N$; when $z_k=0$ it reduces to
$F_{ijk}=\delta(q_i-q_j)z_i z_j/N$.

If $z_4\ne0$, orient $z_4>0$. The three tests with $k=4$
give $z_1\geq z_2\geq z_3$. If $z_1>z_2$, the remaining
$G_{123}\leq0$ forces $z_3\geq0$, contradicting the zero bath
mean. Therefore $z_1=z_2=a$.
Now $F_{124}\leq0$ gives $0\leq a\leq z_4$.
The zero bath mean then forces $z_3<0$, and $F_{123}\leq0$
forces $a=0$. Hence $z$ is supported exactly on states $3,4$.

If $z_4=0$ and $z_3\ne0$, orient $z_3>0$.
Then $G_{123}\leq0$ gives $z_1\geq z_2$; the zero bath mean
implies $z_2<0$. The test $F_{124}\leq0$ now gives $z_1\geq0$,
whereas $F_{134}\leq0$ gives $z_1\leq0$.
Thus $z_1=0$ and the support is exactly states $2,3$.
Finally, if $z_4=z_3=0$, the nonzero zero-mean vector $z$ is
supported exactly on states $1,2$.

For support $(k,k+1)$ choose the normalization
$z_k=-1/q_k$, $z_{k+1}=1/q_{k+1}$.
Then $-Dzz^\top/N=J^{(k)}$ with the rates displayed in the theorem.
This proves necessity and uniqueness of the pair. It also shows that
these generators have strictly positive off-diagonal rates, even if
only nonnegative rates were assumed initially.

For completeness, write $d_k=\delta/(q_k+q_{k+1})$. The nonzero
triplet evaluations for the converse are given below; all omitted
evaluations are zero.
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
\text{pair}&F\text{ values}&G\text{ values}\\ \hline
12&F_{123}=F_{124}=-(q_1-q_2)d_1&\text{none}\\[2pt]
23&\begin{gathered}F_{123}=-(q_2+q_3/2)d_2\\
F_{234}=-(q_2-q_3)d_2\end{gathered}&G_{123}=-q_2d_2\\[2pt]
34&F_{134}=F_{234}=-(q_3+q_4/2)d_3&G_{134}=G_{234}=-q_3d_3
\end{array}
\]
The extra matrix $J^{(k)}$ has eigenvalue $-1$ on its fast
right eigenvector $Dz$ and vanishes on its three-dimensional kernel; $R$ is
$-I$ on the zero-sum probability-perturbation subspace. This proves the claimed spectrum.
If the three nonzero eigenvalues coincide, the reversible spectral
resolution directly gives $M=\rho R$.

To check stochastic monotonicity directly, let the adjacent differences
of a function be $d_i=f_{i+1}-f_i$. The backward reset generator acts
on these differences as $-I$. For $J^{(k)}$, its adjacent-difference
matrix has only column $k$ nonzero: the diagonal entry is $-1$,
the entry in row $k-1$, when present, is $q_{k+1}/(q_k+q_{k+1})$,
and the entry in row $k+1$, when present, is
$q_k/(q_k+q_{k+1})$. Therefore the adjacent-difference matrix for
$M$ is Metzler. The earlier reversible stochastic-order argument gives
the all-time convex-divergence ordering. Strict convexity makes its
supporting-line inequality strict for distinct evolved distributions;
$e^{tM}$ is invertible at finite $t$, so distinct Gibbs preparations
remain distinct. This proves the asserted strictness without any
spectral simplicity assumption.
\end{proof}

The classification also proves that strict failure of all eight linear
tests forces a simple slow mode: every repeated-slow generator above
has several triplet equalities. This is a mathematical classification
within the stated premise, not a priority claim. The exact symbolic
identities, converse tables, spectra and adjacent-difference matrices
are checked in \texttt{research/full\_triplet\_degenerate\_certificate.py}.
