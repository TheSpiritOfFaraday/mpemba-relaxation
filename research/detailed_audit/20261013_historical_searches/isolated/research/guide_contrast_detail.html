Slow route: weighted coordinates explain the sign of the slow overlap

1. Replace a difficult average with simple comparisons

Here q is the bath probability vector: each qᵢ > 0 and their sum is one. Define Qₖ = q₁+⋯+qₖ. A prefix contrast compares the first k states with the next state:

h(k) = (1, …, 1, −Qₖ/qₖ₊₁, 0, …, 0).

There are k initial ones. The bath-weighted average is Qₖ−qₖ₊₁(Qₖ/qₖ₊₁) = 0. For four states, these are (1,−q₁/q₂,0,0), (1,1,−Q₂/q₃,0), and (1,1,1,−Q₃/q₄).

The weighted dot product of vectors f and g is Σqᵢfᵢgᵢ. It is the ordinary dot product after multiplying coordinate i by √qᵢ. Any two different prefix contrasts have weighted dot product zero: the longer contrast is constant on the support of the shorter, whose weighted sum is zero. Each squared length is ηₖ = QₖQₖ₊₁/qₖ₊₁. Dividing h(k) by √ηₖ produces a unit vector e(k).

These n−1 nonzero perpendicular vectors give coordinates for every vector with bath mean zero. This is just the familiar idea of perpendicular x and y axes, applied to a space with n−1 directions.

2. Translate the rate inequalities

Detailed balance makes kᵢⱼ = Mᵢⱼ/qᵢ symmetric. The triplet quantity Gᵢⱼₖ is qⱼ(kᵢₖ−kⱼₖ). Thus G ≤ 0 says kᵢₖ ≤ kⱼₖ whenever i < j < k. Write the generator acting on functions as L = Mᵀ. In the unit contrast coordinates its matrix is Aₖₗ = Σqᵢ eᵢ(k)(Le(l))ᵢ. Detailed balance makes A symmetric.

For k < l, direct multiplication gives the following off-diagonal entry:

√(ηₖηₗ) Aₖₗ = Qₗ₊₁ Σᵢ≤ₖ qᵢ(kₖ₊₁,ₗ₊₁−kᵢ,ₗ₊₁)
+ Σⱼ≥ₗ₊₂ qⱼ Σᵢ≤ₖ qᵢ(kₖ₊₁,ⱼ−kᵢⱼ) ≥ 0.

Every displayed difference is nonnegative by G ≤ 0. To reproduce the multiplication, first observe that for i ≤ k+1 ≤ l, (Lh(l))ᵢ = −Qₗ₊₁kᵢ,ₗ₊₁−Σⱼ≥ₗ₊₂qⱼkᵢⱼ. Taking the shorter contrast subtracts the value at k+1 from the weighted prefix values.

3. Why nonnegative coordinates stay nonnegative

The off-diagonal entries of A are nonnegative; its diagonal entries can be negative. Choose c large enough that every entry of A+cI is nonnegative. The exponential series I+t(A+cI)+t²(A+cI)²/2!+⋯ then has nonnegative entries and diagonal entries at least one. Multiplying by e−ct shows etA has nonnegative entries and positive diagonal entries for every finite t ≥ 0. This is a direct reason that componentwise positive vectors remain positive.

The largest-eigenvalue vector of a symmetric A can be chosen nonnegative. One elementary explanation is to maximize xᵀAx over unit vectors. Replacing each coordinate by its absolute value cannot lower that expression, since its off-diagonal coefficients are nonnegative. At a maximum, the usual constrained derivative calculation gives Ax = λx. Maximizing selects the largest eigenvalue. No claim that every eigenvector has one sign is being made.

4. Compute the thermal contrast coordinates

Let gₖ(β) = Σeᵢ(k)πᵢ(β). Apart from the positive factor Z√ηₖ, its numerator is

Nₖ(β) = e−βEₖ₊₁ Σᵢ≤ₖ [eβ(Eₖ₊₁−Eᵢ)−eβb(Eₖ₊₁−Eᵢ)].

All energy gaps in brackets are positive. Thus all coordinates gₖ are negative on cooling, zero at the bath, and positive on heating. A nonzero nonnegative slow eigenvector has strictly negative dot product with g on cooling and strictly positive dot product on heating. That proves the overlap sign used in Step 14.

On heating the coordinates also increase strictly. Write Z = Nₖ+Tₖ, where Tₖ = (1+Qₖ/qₖ₊₁)e−βEₖ₊₁+Σⱼ≥ₖ₊₂e−βEⱼ. Both parts are positive. Their logarithmic derivatives satisfy Nₖ′/Nₖ > −Eₖ₊₁ and Tₖ′/Tₖ ≤ −Eₖ₊₁. The first follows from the increasing positive bracket above; the second is a weighted average of negative energies. The quotient rule now gives (Nₖ/Z)′ > 0.

5. What follows when the slow eigenvalue repeats?

A single overlap is then insufficient. Instead use the projection Π onto the entire slow eigenspace. Symmetric diagonalization gives Π = limt→∞e−λ₂tetA. The matrices inside the limit are nonnegative, so Π is nonnegative too. It is nonzero and has a positive diagonal entry. A vector whose entries are all strictly positive, or all strictly negative, therefore cannot project to zero. This excludes strong effects even with a repeated slow eigenvalue.

For heating, farther preparations have strictly larger positive g coordinates. Applying etA preserves that order. The squared weighted distance is the sum of the squares of these coordinates, so it stays strictly larger at every finite time. The leading KL coefficient is proportional to ‖Πg‖² and also increases on heating. This proves the stated all-time squared-distance and asymptotic KL conclusions. It does not establish all-time KL or total-variation order.
