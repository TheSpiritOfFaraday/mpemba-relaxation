Independent audit: single-endpoint classification under the full triplet premise
2026-10-11. Verifier agent.

OUTCOME
The proposed corollary is valid for the stated four-state, reversible,
positive-finite-bath, SIMPLE-slow-mode domain with all original triplet
predicates failing. Orient w using its nonnegative Helmert coordinates,
so b(beta)<0 on cooling. Then an ordinary asymptotic cooling effect exists
if and only if b'(0)<0. If it exists there is exactly one stationary point
beta_m in (0,beta_b), a strict maximum of |b|, and the interval on which
|b| increases with beta is exactly (0,beta_m). Thus no separate cooling
anomaly interval can begin at a strictly positive beta under this premise.
The beta=0 endpoint is used only for a derivative test, not as an allowed
finite preparation. This is not a finite-time distance classification.

DEPENDENCIES
The independently audited arithmetic lemma gives
  w1>=w2, w1+w2>=2w3, w4<=0.
The contrast theorem gives b<0 on cooling and the derivative-curve identity
is b'=g Phi with g=pi1'>0, Phi=A+B u+C v(u), C=w3-w4. The curve v(u)
is strictly convex, and u decreases from u0=u(0) to -1 as beta increases
from zero to infinity. The finite limiting endpoint is
  Phi(-1)=w1-w2>=0.

The additionally needed strict bath derivative can be checked directly.
Each unnormalized Helmert thermal numerator is
  Nk(beta)=exp(-beta E_(k+1))
    sum_(i<=k)[exp(beta(E_(k+1)-Ei))-exp(beta_b(E_(k+1)-Ei))].
Nk(beta_b)=0 and Nk'(beta_b)>0 since all gaps are positive. Dividing by
the positive partition and norm factors preserves this sign. A nonzero
nonnegative combination therefore has b'(beta_b)>0. Heating monotonicity
alone would not prove strict derivative at its boundary; this explicit
calculation supplies it.

CASE 1: C>=0
Write b=P m+(1-P)w4, where m is the conditional expectation on states1-3
and P their Gibbs mass. The arithmetic lemma implies
  m(0)=(w1+w2+w3)/3>=w3>=w4,
and m is nondecreasing for beta>=0. Also P'>0. Hence
  b'=P m'+P'(m-w4)>=0.
Strictness holds everywhere: if the first three entries are not identical,
their decomposition into the two established positive-derivative contrasts
gives m'>0 at every finite beta>=0. If they are identical, m is constant,
and m>w4 unless the entire eigenvector is constant, which is excluded.
Thus b'>0 on this whole domain, including beta=0. No cooling anomaly occurs.

CASE 2: C<0
Phi(u) is strictly concave on -1<u<u0 and continuous at the two limiting
endpoints. If Phi(u0)>=0, both endpoint values are nonnegative. Strict
concavity gives Phi(u)>0 at every interior point. This includes the cases
Phi(u0)=0 and/or Phi(-1)=0: endpoint equality creates no interior negative
interval and no finite stationary point. Consequently no effect occurs.

If Phi(u0)<0, the bath value Phi(ub)>0 forces a root between ub and u0.
On the interval from -1 to ub, concavity and the nonnegative/positive endpoint
values make Phi strictly positive. To the right of ub, a concave function
that ends negative has exactly one zero: after crossing from positive to
negative its chord slopes cannot increase enough to cross back. Equivalently,
its nonnegative superlevel set is an interval, and strict concavity excludes
a zero plateau. Thus there is exactly one root u_m in (ub,u0). Since u
decreases with beta, Phi is negative for 0<=beta<beta_m and positive for
all beta>beta_m. At the root its slope in u is strictly negative (bounded
above by the negative secant slope from ub), so the zero is simple.

Because b<0 on cooling, |b|'=-b'. The unique root is therefore the strict
cooling maximum and the anomalous derivative interval is (0,beta_m).
Any two sufficiently close finite positive temperatures inside this interval
give a strict cooling witness. Conversely, all other endpoint/sign cases
have b'>0 throughout the open cooling domain and admit no such pair.

UNIFORM COVARIANCE FORM
At beta=0 the Gibbs distribution is uniform, so the criterion is equivalently
  Cov_uniform(w,E)>0,
since b'(0)=-Cov_uniform(w,E). The bath/generator/eigenvector remain fixed
while differentiating. The orientation of w must be the specified one;
reversing it reverses this raw derivative sign. No derivative scan or auxiliary
thermal root is needed to decide existence, although determining beta_m itself
still requires solving b'(beta_m)=0.

SCOPE AND EQUALITY AUDIT
- The equality b'(0)=0 excludes an effect; it is not an undecided threshold.
- Equality w1=w2 is allowed; Phi(infinity)=0 causes no extra finite root.
- C=0 belongs to the first case and has strictly positive derivative.
- Nonnegative Helmert coordinates may contain zeros; the arguments need only
  a nonzero simple mode and the already proved arithmetic inequalities.
- No claim is made for a degenerate slow space or for generators outside the
  full F/G premise. The more general endpoint/extremum classifier remains
  necessary for those broader simple-mode generators.

No sign or concavity-boundary counterexample survives these implications.
No additional numerical sampling was needed: the two cases exhaust the sign
of C and retain every endpoint equality explicitly.
