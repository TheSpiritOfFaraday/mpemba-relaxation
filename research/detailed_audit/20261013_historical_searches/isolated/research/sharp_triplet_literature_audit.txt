RESULT AND RECOMMENDED CLAIM
The primary sources inspected do not state the exact sharp 25% amplitude bound
or the full-triplet four-state endpoint criterion proved in this project. This
is a bounded negative search result, not proof of publication priority.
The repeated-slow theorem needs a narrower novelty claim: its explicit family
already belongs to the September 2026 paper's single-pair deviation construction.
The additional result here is necessity: every reversible four-state generator
with all original triplet failures and exactly one faster nonzero mode must be
reset plus an accelerated adjacent pair. The all-time divergence conclusion is
also an additional argument, not a claim that reset or pair perturbations are new.

PRECISE PROJECT RESULTS BEING COMPARED
A. Four distinct energies, positive finite bath inverse temperature, positive
reversible rates, simple slow mode, and all original triplet predicates false:
for any anomalous finite cooling pair, the relative slow-amplitude gain
  1 - |b(beta_h)|/|b(beta_c)|
is strictly below 1/4; its supremum over the family is 1/4. Equivalently the
reverse amplitude ratio is below 4/3. This is not a 25% relaxation-time bound.
B. In the same family, orient the left slow eigenvector to make b negative on
cooling. Ordinary asymptotic cooling exists iff Cov_uniform(w,E)>0, equivalently
b'(0)<0. There is then exactly one positive turning point, before the bath.
The analytic beta=0 endpoint is not itself a permitted finite-temperature witness.
C. If the slow eigenvalue has multiplicity two, the full premise forces
  M = rho R + delta J^(k),  rho,delta>0, k=1,2,3,
where R=q 1^T-I and J^(k) exchanges only adjacent states k,k+1 with equilibrium
weights q. Triple degeneracy gives pure reset. These generators preserve order
and yield all-time ordering of convex divergences for Gibbs preparations.

2. David Gelbwaser-Klimovsky,
   Efficient protocol for the Markovian Mpemba effect in N-level systems.
   https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09988
   https://arxiv.org/html/2609.09988v1
   Live submission history: v1 only, 2026-09-09 10:15:30 UTC.
   Section II gives the reset singular point and the single-pair states deviation
   (SPSD): multiply one forward/reverse rate pair by 1+Delta. Its triplet analysis
   implies that a positive deviation on an adjacent pair keeps all triplets
   failing. It continues to assert the N-level triplet necessity. No exhaustive
   characterization of all repeated-slow four-state generators, sharp 25% bound,
   or four-state uniform-covariance equivalence was located.
   Exact comparison (our algebra): with normalized q and reset rate rho, its
   adjacent-pair SPSD is rho R + delta J^(k), where
     delta = rho Delta (q_k+q_(k+1)).
   Thus the constructed family itself must be credited to this prior work;
   exhaustiveness under full F/G is the separate project theorem.

covariance plus uniform or infinite temperature; endpoint criteria; amplitude
ratios/upper bounds/25%/quarter/4/3; rank-one or one-fast-mode generators;

2603.04567 with correction/counterexample. Twenty-two query strings were run

rereading their locally cached full texts. Search results about unrelated
quantum, non-Markovian, or empirical Mpemba effects were not treated as evidence
for this finite reversible original-rate problem. Secondary summaries were not
used to establish a literature claim.

erratum search, or a check of unpublished manuscripts. Search-engine absence
cannot establish priority. The most defensible report wording is:
  "The primary sources checked on 11 October 2026 do not state these precise
  sharp-bound and endpoint theorems. We prove them here under the original
  triplet premise; publication priority remains unverified. The reset plus
  adjacent-pair family is a special case of the prior SPSD construction; our
  repeated-slow result proves its necessity under that premise."
