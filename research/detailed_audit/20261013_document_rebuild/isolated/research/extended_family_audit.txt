1. INVERSE REALIZABILITY: BOTH DIRECTIONS
For normalized q and q-centered nonzero w, the two columns of U span the
orthogonal complement of 1,w under the D inner product. Thus D^(1/2)U has
rank two, is orthogonal to sqrt(q),D^(1/2)w, and
  D^(-1/2) M0 D^(1/2)=-D^(1/2) U B U^T D^(1/2).
For B positive definite this has exactly two strictly negative eigenvalues
and the two-dimensional stated kernel. Adding rho(q 1^T-I) shifts only
the nonstationary spectrum, producing a simple slow mode w. Large rho
makes every off-diagonal entry positive. Reset invariance of F,G is exact,
so original predicates fail after positivity makes the branch lemma applicable.

Conversely, M0=M+lambda2(q 1^T-I) has eigenvalues zero on the stationary
and slow spaces and lambda_j-lambda2<0 on the two faster spaces. Its negative
restriction can be represented uniquely as U B U^T after the D similarity,
with B positive definite. Homogeneous triplet inequalities survive the reset
and positive scaling. Trace normalization gives the open disk exactly.

The nearest-point enumeration for rational half-planes is complete. A
nonempty closed polyhedron in R^2 has a unique norm minimizer: it is the
origin, a projection onto one active line, or a vertex from two independent
active lines. Degenerate line/point intersections are included. Each candidate
and squared norm is rational. A minimum equal to one is correctly excluded.
The constructed rho exceeds every off-diagonal entry of UBU^T, giving
strictly positive rates in both directions. The det/trace gap lower bound
is positive because U^TDU and B are positive definite.

IMPLEMENTATION SCOPE ISSUE (reported promptly)
exact_realize(...,slack>0) replaces c+a x+b y<=0 by a stronger inequality
c+a x+b y+slack*max(|c|,|a|,|b|)<=0. A feasible answer still gives a valid
original realization, but feasible=False does not exclude feasibility at
slack=0. Therefore the exact completeness claim must be restricted to
slack=0, and nonzero-slack failures labeled as failures of the strengthened
problem. The optional slack should also be checked/converted as a nonnegative
rational if the interface promises no floating comparisons. The default
zero-slack mathematical classification is unaffected.

2. THE EXPLICIT FAST BASIS AND CONTINUOUS CERTIFICATE
Independently substituted the displayed U into q^T U=0 and w^T D U=0.
Both identities hold. Its third/fourth-coordinate identity block proves
rank two. Its entries agree algebraically with the nullspace basis recorded
by the certificate. The two equations used in the code are precisely rows
F124 and G234 in lexicographic triplet order.

The Bernstein checker correctly converts a power polynomial p(t) of degree n
to coefficients b_k=sum_{j<=k} a_j binom(k,j)/binom(n,j). All-positive
numerator and denominator Bernstein coefficients imply a strictly positive
rational function for 0<t<=1. Removing powers of epsilon is legitimate
because epsilon>0. Flipping both numerator and denominator when needed
preserves the ratio. The nonzero coefficient determinant is separately
certified, so the linear solution has no hidden singularity in the interval.

The receipt contains 30 checks: two for B, one determinant, twelve rates,
six nonzero negative original F/G expressions, one spectral bound and eight
strict auxiliary F/G expressions. The two equality expressions are exact
rational identities, rather than uncertain zero tests. This is a continuous
interval argument, not a finite parameter sample. These observations audit
the proof mechanism; they do not replace the recorded rational coefficients.

3. UNIFORM SLOW SIMPLICITY AND THE STRICT PERTURBATION
The two fast increments are the positive eigenvalues of BJ/epsilon^2,
where J=U^TDU is positive definite. BJ is similar to a positive definite
symmetric matrix. Their product/trace is therefore strictly smaller than
the lesser increment. The certified product/trace >1/3 proves the uniform
slow gap and spectrum 0,-3,-3-nu1,-3-nu2.

For H with reduced conductances bounded by 100, the symmetric Dirichlet form
is bounded by 100 Var_q(f); hence the symmetric perturbation epsilon^4 H
has operator norm at most delta=100 epsilon^4. It preserves the stationary
vector. Weyl gives a new slow-fast gap >1/3-2 delta>1/4 throughout
0<epsilon<=1/30. Adding epsilon^4 H turns the two equalities and the six
already strict inequalities into eight strict negative F/G expressions.
All original rates also remain strictly positive.

For the eigenvector estimate, resolve a perturbed unit symmetric slow vector
into the old slow direction plus its fast orthogonal complement. Projection
of its eigen equation bounds the latter by delta/(gap-delta). With common
orientation, the full unit-vector difference is at most sqrt(2) times this.
Rescaling both to the same original weighted norm is harmless, and
||w||_q<=1 since every displayed component has magnitude <=1. Even using
3/2 instead of sqrt(2), the coefficient obeys
  (3/2)*100/(1/3-100/30^4) < 500.
Thus the weighted difference is <500 epsilon^4. Since sum(qhat)<3 and
each qhat_i>=epsilon^2, q_i>=epsilon^2/3. Consequently the coordinate error
is <500 sqrt(3) epsilon^3<1000 epsilon^3, uniformly for all four entries.
Every Gibbs overlap error is bounded by the maximum coordinate error.
This normalization is consistent for both temperatures; ratios do not depend
on the common eigenvector scale. It does not assert wtilde_1=1.

4. THE QUARTER LIMIT AND FINITE WITNESS
For L=log(1/epsilon), beta_h=L^-2 gives exp(-2L beta_h)->1, whereas
beta_c=L^-1/2 gives exp(-2L beta_c)->0. At both preparations the first
three weights tend to one. Their limiting slow components sum to -2/15.
The resulting overlaps are -1/30 and -2/45, respectively, with ratio 3/4.
Their limits are nonzero; the uniform O(epsilon^3) perturbation error cannot
change the limit or eventual strict ordering. For L>=log 30 both temperatures
are finite, positive, less than one, and beta_h<beta_c.

The nth-root helper bounds each q_i^(1/n) by exact integer-power bisection
on a 10^-12 grid. Signed numerator terms and positive denominator corners
give a valid overlap enclosure. For negative overlaps, the gain interval
uses largest hot magnitude/smallest cold magnitude for its lower endpoint,
and the converse for its upper endpoint. The thermal checker uses exactly
these directions and enlarges both intervals by the proved perturbation error.
No thermal estimate is inferred from a floating-point root.

Independently compared the receipt's rational gain endpoints with the printed
decimal bounds: both displayed inequalities round outward, namely
  22.6171597478 < 100*gain_lower <= 100*gain_upper < 22.6171597506.
The finite perturbed claim is >22%, rather than the entire narrow unperturbed
interval, as justified by the checker. At epsilon=10^-50 all energies and
initial temperatures remain finite.

The KL asymptotic advantage is 1-(3/4)^2=7/16. With slow rate tending to
3, the asymptotic time advance is log(4/3)/3. Neither quantity equals a
universal percentage reduction of a finite cooling time.

5. INDEPENDENT SIGN ARGUMENT FOR THE PEAK
An elementary check avoids relying on an external contrast-cone result.
The unnormalized bath-weighted prefixes of w are
  S1=1, S2=1/5+4 epsilon/15, S3=epsilon^3, S4=0.
All first three are positive. For beta<1 the Gibbs-to-bath likelihood ratio
strictly increases in energy, so summation by parts gives b(beta)<0.
At the bath,
  b'(1)=sum_{k=1}^3 S_k(E_{k+1}-E_k)/sum(qhat)>0.
Thus R=|b|=-b throughout cooling. This confirms the absolute-amplitude sign
used in the expansion and the endpoint derivative at the bath.

The leading ratio R0=(y/3-1/5)/(2+y+x), y=exp(-a beta),x=exp(-2L beta),
approximates R uniformly on beta in [0,1] with error O(epsilon). Indeed
d^beta=1+O(epsilon), r=-1/3+O(epsilon), and the fourth numerator term is
-epsilon x. Its derivative error is O(L epsilon), which is exponentially
smaller than the algebraic orders retained below.

Independently differentiating gives, after multiplying the derivative
numerator by 15,
  -13 a y + x[5(2L-a)y-6L].
Initially R'>0 for large L; at beta=1, R'=-b'<0. The two-stationary-point
bound counts multiplicities: a single sign change is forced, and any extra
simple crossing would have the wrong endpoint parity, while a tangency or
triple zero would exceed the bound. Hence there is exactly one cooling
stationary point, a strict amplitude maximum.

The maximum tends to beta=0: at every fixed positive beta the limiting
amplitude is strictly smaller than 2/45, whereas the beta_c=L^-1/2 sequence
attains 2/45 in the limit. Also L beta_m must diverge; otherwise the positive
normalization derivative term is order L and cannot balance the bounded
negative numerator derivative. The stationary equation therefore first yields
x~13a/(4L) and beta_m~log L/(2L). Substitution back sharpens it to
  x=13a/(4L)[1+O(beta_m+1/L)],
  beta_m=log(4L/(13a))/(2L)+O(log L/L^2).
The O(L epsilon) derivative residual changes x only by O(epsilon), negligible
relative to 1/L and to all stated algebraic errors.

Finally expansion of the ratio gives
  R(beta_m)=(2/45)[1-(13a/6)beta_m-x/3
                   +O(beta_m^2+beta_m x+x^2+epsilon)],
  R(0)=1/30+O(epsilon).
Thus 1-R(0)/R(beta_m)=1/4-(13a/8)beta_m-x/4 plus the same quadratic
orders. Substitution produces precisely
  1/4 - (13a/(16L))[log(4L/(13a))+1]
       + O((log L)^2/L^2).
Both the numerical coefficient and error order in the report are correct.
The infinite-temperature reference is explicitly a limit; finite sufficiently
hot preparations can approach it. The result asserts attainability of this
quarter limit, not global optimality under the triplet constraints.

6. LIMITATIONS OF THIS AUDIT
The proofs rely on exact symbolic simplification/Bernstein arithmetic in the
continuous checker, previously generated rational receipts, and the stated
Chebyshev stationary-point bound audited separately. No independent second
implementation of the entire continuous certificate was run, and no claim
of publication novelty was assessed in this audit. The only identified issue
is the optional-slack API scope described above, not the mathematical theorem
or the accepted family/counterexample.
