Lesson 33. A rate is not a probability: build the clock first

Begin with units.

A probability is a number between zero and one. A rate has units of inverse time. A rate of three events per second is perfectly meaningful; a probability of three is not. If an event has constant rate rr, its probability of occurring in a very short interval of length hh is rh+o(h)rh+o(h). The notation o(h)o(h) means that the error divided by hh tends to zero as hh tends to zero. It does not mean that the approximation is exact for a one-second interval.

Derive the waiting law.

Let S(t)S(t) be the probability that no event has happened by time tt. Assume that, conditional on no event so far, the chance in the next short interval is still rh+o(h)rh+o(h). Then

S(t+h)=S(t)(1−rh+o(h)),S′(t)=−rS(t),S(0)=1.S(t+h)=S(t)(1-rh+o(h)),\qquad S'(t)=-rS(t),\qquad S(0)=1.
The function with this derivative and initial value is S(t)=e−rtS(t)=e^{-rt}. Thus the probability of at least one event by time tt is 1−e−rt1-e^{-rt}. For r=2r=2 per second and t=0.1t=0.1 seconds, this is 1−e−0.21-e^{-0.2}, approximately 0.18130.1813. The short-time estimate rt=0.2rt=0.2 is close but different. For t=1t=1 second the estimate rt=2rt=2 is not a probability at all.

Why the mean wait is 1/r1/r.

The density of the first event time is re−rtre^{-rt}. Its mean is

∫0∞tre−rtdt=[−te−rt]0∞+∫0∞e−rtdt=1r.\int_0^\infty t r e^{-rt}\,dt =[-te^{-rt}]_0^\infty+\int_0^\infty e^{-rt}\,dt=\frac1r.
The integration by parts has merely moved the factor tt onto a derivative. There is also a geometric interpretation: the mean waiting time is the area under the survival curve S(t)S(t).

Several possible exits.

From state jj, let the exit rates to other states be aija_{ij}. Independent exponential clocks compete. The probability that none has rung by tt is the product ∏i≠je−aijt\prod_{i\ne j}e^{-a_{ij}t}, hence the total exit rate is rj=∑i≠jaijr_j=\sum_{i\ne j}a_{ij}. Conditional on an exit, its destination is ii with probability aij/rja_{ij}/r_j. This produces the diagonal entry Mjj=−rjM_{jj}=-r_j and explains why a column sums to zero. The negative diagonal does not describe a negative probability; it describes probability leaving.

Try it.

Suppose the rates from one state to two others are 22 and 33 per second. The mean residence time is 1/51/5 second, and the next destination probabilities are 2/52/5 and 3/53/5. Multiplying all rates by ten divides every waiting time by ten. It does not change these destination probabilities or the equilibrium distribution. Consequently, a percentage time advantage alone supplies no absolute number of seconds without a physical rate calibration.

Lesson 34. Replace unequal clocks by one clock and harmless pauses

A deliberately simple two-state example.

Use a column probability vector and let

M=(−212−1),ρ=2,P=I+M/ρ=(01/211/2).M=\begin{pmatrix}-2&1\\2&-1\end{pmatrix},\qquad \rho=2,\qquad P=I+M/\rho=\begin{pmatrix}0&1/2\\1&1/2\end{pmatrix}.
The first state exits at rate 22; the second exits at rate 11. Instead ring a clock of rate 22 in either state. At each ring, choose the next state using the current column of PP. In state two, half the rings leave the state unchanged. Its actual transition rate is therefore 2×(1/2)=12\times(1/2)=1, exactly as required. The pauses are real elapsed time.

Generalize only after the example.

Choose ρ\rho at least as large as every exit rate. Then P=I+M/ρP=I+M/\rho has nonnegative entries and unit column sums. Such a matrix is called stochastic: it sends probability vectors to probability vectors. The identity M=ρ(P−I)M=\rho(P-I) gives

eMt=eρt(P−I)=e−ρt∑k=0∞(ρt)kk!Pk.e^{Mt}=e^{\rho t(P-I)} =e^{-\rho t}\sum_{k=0}^\infty\frac{(\rho t)^k}{k!}P^k.
The factorization is allowed because PP commutes with II. It would not be legitimate to split an arbitrary matrix exponential into two factors without checking commutation.

Read every factor as a probability.

Write μ=ρt\mu=\rho t. A Poisson random variable NN has probabilities Pr⁡(N=k)=e−μμk/k!\Pr(N=k)=e^{-\mu}\mu^k/k!. Here NN counts clock rings by time tt. Conditional on N=kN=k, the distribution is Pkp(0)P^kp(0). Averaging these conditional distributions gives the displayed formula. This procedure is called uniformization. It is an exact continuous-time construction, not a small-time-step approximation.

At μ=1\mu=1, the probabilities of zero, one and two rings are respectively e−1e^{-1}, e−1e^{-1} and e−1/2e^{-1}/2. Their sum is about 0.91970.9197. The missing 0.08030.0803 is the probability of three or more rings; it cannot simply be discarded when a rigorous error bound is required.

Check the two-state equilibrium.

Solving Mπ=0M\pi=0 and π1+π2=1\pi_1+\pi_2=1 gives π=(1/3,2/3)⊤\pi=(1/3,2/3)^\top. Starting from (1,0)⊤(1,0)^\top, the exact first component is p1(t)=1/3+(2/3)e−3tp_1(t)=1/3+(2/3)e^{-3t} and the second is its complement. Differentiating verifies p1′=1−3p1p'_1=1-3p_1, which is also the first row of MpMp. This small solvable model is a useful check of the matrix convention, the clock interpretation and any implementation of uniformization.

Lesson 35. Bound the part of an infinite sum that you did not compute

First learn the geometric comparison.

For 0≤q<10\leq q<1, multiplying 1+q+q2+⋯1+q+q^2+\cdots by 1−q1-q cancels every term except the first, giving 1/(1−q)1/(1-q). If a nonnegative series starts with aa and every later term is at most qq times its predecessor, its whole sum is at most a/(1−q)a/(1-q). Equality is unnecessary; an upper bound suffices.

For Poisson weights wk=e−μμk/k!w_k=e^{-\mu}\mu^k/k!,

wk+1wk=μk+1.\frac{w_{k+1}}{w_k}=\frac{\mu}{k+1}.
After keeping terms 00 through KK, the first omitted weight is wK+1w_{K+1}. Every subsequent ratio is at most q=μ/(K+2)q=\mu/(K+2). Provided q<1q<1,
RK=∑k=K+1∞wk≤wK+11−μ/(K+2).R_K=\sum_{k=K+1}^\infty w_k \leq\frac{w_{K+1}}{1-\mu/(K+2)}.
The K+2K+2 in the denominator is intentional: it bounds the ratio from the first omitted term to the next omitted term. Confusing it with the first omitted term’s index is an easy off-by-one mistake.

Work a numerical example.

Take μ=1\mu=1 and keep K=2K=2. Then w3=e−1/6w_3=e^{-1}/6 and q=1/4q=1/4, so R2≤2e−1/9R_2\leq 2e^{-1}/9, approximately 0.081750.08175. The actual missing mass is approximately 0.080300.08030, below the bound. The bound is a little wider than necessary, but its validity is transparent.

Turn a scalar tail into a vector guarantee.

Every component of Pkp(0)P^kp(0) lies in [0,1][0,1]. If sis_i is the computed partial sum for component ii, then pi(t)∈[si,si+RK]p_i(t)\in[s_i,s_i+R_K]. These independent intervals need not sum to exactly one; they are an enclosure of a probability vector, not themselves a new probability vector. Renormalizing their endpoints without a proof could invalidate the enclosure.

Include arithmetic error as well as truncation error.

An exact tail formula does not excuse rounding errors in the partial sum. The nominal checker computes interval bounds for inputs, exponentials, matrix products and logarithms. If the entire probability interval is positive, it can enclose each term pilog⁡(pi/πi)p_i\log(p_i/\pi_i) and hence KL. An interval crossing zero requires a valid alternative treatment or a failure to certify; a logarithm of an invalid endpoint is not evidence.

In the replayed nominal calculation, at most 281 terms were needed and the largest reported tail upper bound was below 10−4510^{-45}. The small tail is one part of the certificate. The validity of PP, the input correspondence, the outward arithmetic and the later comparison inequalities remain separate necessary parts. More printed digits alone establish none of them.

Lesson 36. Prove a whole time interval using its endpoints

A descending staircase picture.

Suppose two distances Dc(t)D_c(t) and Dh(t)D_h(t) are nonincreasing in time. On a cell [a,b][a,b], the cold distance is at least its value at the right endpoint, while the hot distance is at most its value at the left endpoint:

Dc(t)≥Dc(b),Dh(t)≤Dh(a).D_c(t)\geq D_c(b),\qquad D_h(t)\leq D_h(a).
Therefore a single strict inequality
Dh(a)<0.9Dc(b)D_h(a)<0.9D_c(b)
proves Dh(t)<0.9Dc(t)D_h(t)<0.9D_c(t) for every time in that cell. The endpoints are deliberately mismatched to use the worst case for each curve. Merely checking Dh(a)<0.9Dc(a)D_h(a)<0.9D_c(a) and the analogous inequality at bb would not control a crossing between them.

Why KL decreases here.

For a stochastic transition matrix KK and positive equilibrium π\pi, let ri=pi/πir_i=p_i/\pi_i. When Kπ=πK\pi=\pi, the output likelihood ratio is

(Kp)jπj=∑iKjiπiπjri.\frac{(Kp)_j}{\pi_j}=\sum_i\frac{K_{ji}\pi_i}{\pi_j}r_i.
The coefficients in this sum are nonnegative and sum to one. Convexity of f(r)=rlog⁡rf(r)=r\log r says that ff of this weighted average is no greater than the weighted average of ff. Multiply by πj\pi_j, sum over jj, and use ∑jKji=1\sum_jK_{ji}=1 to obtain
D(Kp‖π)≤D(p‖π).D(Kp\Vert\pi)\leq D(p\Vert\pi).
Apply this to K=eM(b−a)K=e^{M(b-a)}. This argument proves the monotonicity used above. It does not say that Dh(t)/Dc(t)D_h(t)/D_c(t) is monotone.

A hand-checkable cell.

Suppose outward bounds give Dh(a)≤0.0080D_h(a)\leq0.0080 and Dc(b)≥0.0090D_c(b)\geq0.0090. Then 0.9Dc(b)−Dh(a)≥0.0001>00.9D_c(b)-D_h(a)\geq0.0001>0. Every interior time passes the ten-percent comparison. If the computed margin interval were [−0.0001,0.0002][-0.0001,0.0002], no conclusion would follow; a narrower cell might resolve it.

Join the cells and check the floor.

A finite list t0<t1<⋯<tNt_0<t_1<\cdots<t_N covers the entire closed interval [t0,tN][t_0,t_N] without gaps. Proving the endpoint inequality on every cell proves the full window. Since cold KL decreases, a lower bound Dc(tN)≥10−3D_c(t_N)\geq10^{-3} also proves that floor throughout the window. The nominal replay used 80 cells and a minimum certified comparison margin greater than 0.00044080.0004408. Its fixed endpoints were approximately 58.8926958.89269 and 117.78539117.78539 in attempt-time units. The script separately encloses the slow time to justify the window-length requirement; a decimal time chosen from a numerical eigenvalue does not become exact merely by being described as a multiple of that numerical estimate.

Lesson 37. Prove a whole band of target distances

Reverse the graph in your mind.

For a positive target δ\delta below the initial distance, define tx(δ)t_x(\delta) as the first time preparation xx reaches that target. A smaller target means getting closer to equilibrium, so it takes longer. Thus if δa<δb\delta_a<\delta_b, then tx(δa)≥tx(δb)t_x(\delta_a)\geq t_x(\delta_b). For the irreducible reversible model and a nonequilibrium preparation, KL strictly decreases until its limiting equilibrium value; positive targets below the initial value have unique crossings.

A root estimate proposes; a sign bracket verifies.

A numerical solver might propose t=10.1234t=10.1234. To verify a bracket [L,U][L,U], enclose D(L)−δD(L)-\delta strictly above zero and D(U)−δD(U)-\delta strictly below zero. Continuity gives a crossing between them; strict decrease gives uniqueness. An approximate residual close to zero is weaker than these two certified signs. We also need 0<L<U0<L<U for the ratio bound below.

Choose the unfavorable corners.

For every δ∈[δa,δb]\delta\in[\delta_a,\delta_b],

th(δ)≤th(δa)≤Uh(δa),tc(δ)≥tc(δb)≥Lc(δb)>0.t_h(\delta)\leq t_h(\delta_a)\leq U_h(\delta_a),\qquad t_c(\delta)\geq t_c(\delta_b)\geq L_c(\delta_b)>0.
Division by a positive denominator preserves the intended order, giving
1−th(δ)tc(δ)≥1−Uh(δa)Lc(δb).1-\frac{t_h(\delta)}{t_c(\delta)} \geq 1-\frac{U_h(\delta_a)}{L_c(\delta_b)}.
If the right side exceeds 0.050.05, every target in that cell has at least five-percent time saving. Notice that the numerator and denominator use opposite ends of the target cell. This conservative mismatch is exactly what covers its interior.

Try the ratio with ordinary numbers.

Suppose the hottest trajectory’s slowest permitted arrival is at most 9090 seconds and the cold trajectory’s fastest permitted arrival is at least 100100 seconds. The saving is at least 1−90/100=10%1-90/100=10\%. If the denominator were instead only known to exceed 9292, the bound would fall to about 2.17%2.17\%, even if all sampled same-target comparisons looked better. The proof must use the bounds it has actually established.

The nominal certificate covers [10−3,10−2][10^{-3},10^{-2}] with 60 target cells. Its conservative lower bound on time saving is approximately 8.12245%8.12245\%. This is a guaranteed lower bound for that nominal model, not the exact minimum. The later eight-obligation certificate handles the full uncertain four-state parameter box. Lesson 38. Derive a transition rate from an energy exchange

Begin with one uphill move.

Let two system energies be Ej=1E_j=1 and Ei=3E_i=3, with barrier energy B=5B=5. Draw a nonnegative reservoir energy YY with density βe−βY\beta e^{-\beta Y}. The move from jj is allowed when 1+Y≥51+Y\geq5, namely when Y≥4Y\geq4. At acceptance, set Y′=Y+1−3=Y−2Y'=Y+1-3=Y-2. It is nonnegative, and Ei+Y′=3+Y−2=1+Y=Ej+YE_i+Y'=3+Y-2=1+Y=E_j+Y: combined energy is conserved. The reverse contact sees the same total energy and can undo the move.

Integrate the acceptance probability.

For any c≥0c\geq0,

Pr⁡(Y≥c)=∫c∞βe−βydy=e−βc.\Pr(Y\geq c)=\int_c^\infty\beta e^{-\beta y}\,dy=e^{-\beta c}.
If contacts for this pair arrive at rate ν0\nu_0, the accepted rate from jj to ii is therefore
aij=ν0e−β(B−Ej).a_{ij}=\nu_0e^{-\beta(B-E_j)}.
The reverse rate is aji=ν0e−β(B−Ei)a_{ji}=\nu_0e^{-\beta(B-E_i)}. Their ratio is e−β(Ei−Ej)e^{-\beta(E_i-E_j)}. Multiplying by Gibbs weights gives the stronger flux identity
aije−βEj=ajie−βEi=ν0e−βB,a_{ij}e^{-\beta E_j}=a_{ji}e^{-\beta E_i} =\nu_0e^{-\beta B},
which is detailed balance before the common normalization is inserted.

Separate an always-required gate from the uphill cost.

Write h=B−max⁡(Ei,Ej)h=B-\max(E_i,E_j). Since B−Ej=h+max⁡(Ei−Ej,0)B-E_j=h+\max(E_i-E_j,0), the rate becomes

aij=ν0e−βhmin⁡{1,e−β(Ei−Ej)}.a_{ij}=\nu_0e^{-\beta h}\min\{1,e^{-\beta(E_i-E_j)}\}.
In the example at β=1\beta=1, the common gate contributes e−2e^{-2}, the uphill acceptance contributes another e−2e^{-2}, and their product is e−4e^{-4}. The reverse move has only the common e−2e^{-2} gate.

Why simulation may omit attempts but not their time.

One can retain only contacts with Y≥hY\geq h. Their clock has rate ν0e−βh\nu_0e^{-\beta h}. Conditional on passing the gate,

Pr⁡(Y−h≥z∣Y≥h)=e−β(h+z)e−βh=e−βz.\Pr(Y-h\geq z\mid Y\geq h) =\frac{e^{-\beta(h+z)}}{e^{-\beta h}}=e^{-\beta z}.
Thus a retained mark is hh plus a fresh exponential energy. This proves the thinning rule used in the event simulation. Counting every attempted contact as an accepted transition would alter the process; ignoring rejected contacts without adjusting the clock would alter physical time.

What this construction establishes.

Under independent fresh thermal marks, independent Poisson pair clocks, constant reservoir density of energy states, and instantaneous updates, the rate identity holds exactly for every allowed barrier and energy. It supplies a stochastic bath interpretation of the same four-state model. It does not identify a material or derive an autonomous microscopic bath. The replayed simulation of 500,000 trajectories per preparation agrees with direct evolution at five times to within 0.001180.00118 per component. That is a diagnostic check of the simulation; the rate identity above is the reason the mathematical generators coincide.

Lesson 39. An intermediate state introduces a second clock

Insert a waiting room between two wells.

Suppose the desired well-to-well rates are uu from jj to ii and vv from ii to jj. Insert state ss. Use entrance rates 2u2u from jj and 2v2v from ii, and exit rate bb from ss to each well. On entering ss, the next well is selected with probabilities 1/21/2 and 1/21/2. Half the visits return to the well just left.

If the clock is stopped during every visit to ss, the successful transition rate from jj to ii is 2u×(1/2)=u2u\times(1/2)=u. This is the trace clock: only time actually spent in the wells is retained. With ordinary physical time, each visit adds a mean wait 1/(2b)1/(2b). Those waits cannot be removed when claiming a physical completion time.

Derive the block identity one entry at a time.

Order the wells as (j,i)(j,i). Their direct block, the entrance block, the exit block and the intermediate block are

Qww=(−2u00−2v),Qsw=(2u2v),Qws=(bb),Qss=−2b.Q_{ww}=\begin{pmatrix}-2u&0\\0&-2v\end{pmatrix},\quad Q_{sw}=\begin{pmatrix}2u&2v\end{pmatrix},\quad Q_{ws}=\begin{pmatrix}b\\b\end{pmatrix},\quad Q_{ss}=-2b.
The subscripts give destination first, as everywhere else. Multiplication now gives
Qww−QwsQss−1Qsw=(−2u00−2v)+(uvuv)=(−uvu−v).Q_{ww}-Q_{ws}Q_{ss}^{-1}Q_{sw} =\begin{pmatrix}-2u&0\\0&-2v\end{pmatrix} +\begin{pmatrix}u&v\\u&v\end{pmatrix} =\begin{pmatrix}-u&v\\u&-v\end{pmatrix}.
This is exactly the intended two-well generator. Adding the six pair contributions gives the four-well identity in the technical report. This exact algebra identifies the trace generator; it does not assert that the physical-time well probabilities solve that same four-state equation.

Equilibrium and preparation also change.

In the expanded model, each intermediate has energy BB and phase-space weight η>0\eta>0. Its equilibrium weight is ηe−B\eta e^{-B}, compared with e−Eje^{-E_j} for a well. Taking b=2/ηb=2/\eta in attempt-time units gives mean intermediate residence time η/4\eta/4 and matched equilibrium fluxes. At preparation temperature TT, all ten states must be prepared with weights gae−Ea/Tg_a e^{-E_a/T}. Preparing only the wells would be a different experiment. The phase-space weight η\eta need not be an integer count.

At η=0.05\eta=0.05, the nominal cold and hot preparations place about 1.90%1.90\% and 4.07%4.07\% of their mass in intermediates. Full ten-state calculations retain those populations and every residence time. Their finite-time advantage persists at the tested points. The expanded system also has higher energies and faster rates outside the original all-state caps. It is a separate test of a modeling assumption, not a new certificate inside the original constrained four-state class.

Lesson 40. Read a computational result at the right strength

Four questions before accepting a result.

What object was calculated? What inputs were used? Which errors were bounded? Which variables were covered continuously? An answer such as “70 digits” only addresses numerical precision, and even then does not itself establish a bound on every implementation or modeling error.

The independent ten-state calculation directly constructs and exponentiates the full generator at 70-digit working precision. It agrees with the fast spectral implementation within 6.55×10−136.55\times10^{-13} in KL at five tested times. Different formulas agreeing is useful evidence against a numerical implementation mistake. Neither implementation uses outward error bounds for that comparison, so it is a high-precision diagnostic, not an interval certificate.

Interpret a large sample precisely.

For each of three fixed η\eta values, the study tests 256 corners and 500 seeded interior parameter points. The total is 3(256+500)=22683(256+500)=2268. Each point is further tested on finite target and time grids. No sampled case fails the declared benchmarks. This says exactly what happened on those grids. It does not exclude a narrow failure between points, between time samples, between targets, or at an untested value of η\eta. The continuous four-state certificate cannot be transferred to this ten-state extension merely because their trace generators agree.

A percentage needs a denominator and a clock.

At η=0.05\eta=0.05 and target 10−310^{-3}, the independently computed arrival times are approximately 210.67249210.67249 for cold and 190.05131190.05131 for hot. Using the cold time as denominator gives

210.67249−190.05131210.67249≈0.0978826,\frac{210.67249-190.05131}{210.67249}\approx0.0978826,
or about 9.78826%9.78826\%. Dividing by the hot time would answer a different percentage question. These are attempt-time units, not calibrated seconds. The calculation includes post-quench intermediate residence times but does not include the cost or duration of preparing the hotter initial state.

Know what a program’s success message establishes.

A zero exit code means that the process completed without a reported execution failure. Some exploratory programs record failures in JSON without returning a nonzero code. Read the recorded failure counts, input hashes, unresolved cells, parameter scope and numerical residuals. A certificate runner must also reject incomplete coverage. A numerical search that found nothing has a different meaning from a complete proof that nothing exists.

The final handoff to advanced mathematics.

You can now read the physical section by following a chain of explicit claims: construct valid rates; establish equilibrium; evolve preparations; bound numerical error; prove continuous comparisons; and interpret the clock and physical assumptions. The advanced spectral and interval methods make that chain efficient over an uncertain parameter box. They do not replace any link. When a later formula looks forbidding, identify which link it serves before trying to manipulate its symbols.
