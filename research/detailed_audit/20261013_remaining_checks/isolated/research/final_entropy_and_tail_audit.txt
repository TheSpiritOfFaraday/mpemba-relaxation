1. near_bath_entropy_window.tex

The signed coordinate representation is correct on both sides of the bath.
For heating, the positive numerator is delta times an integral of exp(-delta*s);
for cooling, the outward positive coordinate uses exp(+delta*s). The integral
logarithmic derivative and partition-function logarithmic derivative lie in the
same signed interval of width c, so their difference is in [-c,c], not [-2c,2c].
Consequently y0/x0 is between 1/delta-c and 1/delta+c for either sign.
The nonnegative contrast semigroup preserves these coordinatewise bounds.

The stated angle estimate follows by the indicated quadratic moment inequality;
its minimum is 4ab/(a+b)^2, giving cosine >=sqrt(1-z^2). The density interval has
endpoint ratio exp(z), contains1 by its weighted mean, and is preserved by the
backward Markov semigroup. Every straight-segment density used for the integrated
Hessian remains in this interval. Orthonormality in D gives the exact Hessian
bounds (1/r_plus)I <= T <= (1/r_minus)I. Gradient integration introduces one
factor sigma and differentiation in outward delta introduces the other, so the
cooling sign cancels correctly. The derivative is y^T T x on both sides.

The center-radius operator bound then yields the displayed lower bound.
For 0<z<=2/sqrt5<1, sqrt(1-z^2)>=z/2>tanh(z/2), including strictness at the
upper endpoint. Finite-time x and y are nonzero. The bath endpoint is handled
by equilibrium divergence0 and injectivity of the finite-time exponential,
not by falsely asserting a positive derivative at delta0.
The final arbitrary-heating condition using evolved kappa is also justified:

2. tridiagonal_contrast_entropy.tex

The initial unnormalized coordinate numerator is delta times the truncated
integral of the common positive measure Q(s)exp(-delta*s) ds. Extending the upper
endpoint strictly raises its mean s, so the positive logarithmic thermal slopes
are decreasing in contrast index. Positive diagonal rescaling between normalized
and unnormalized Helmert bases does not change these ratios.

For a Metzler tridiagonal matrix, its second additive compound has nonnegative
off-diagonals in ordered wedge coordinates: an allowed adjacent index move cannot
cross the other occupied index. Thus the exponential has all ordered2x2 minors
nonnegative, including reducible chains. The paired-column determinant expansion
has the correct sign and preserves the decreasing y/x order.

The prefix identity S_k=sum_{j<=k}x_j g_j is exact: all higher unnormalized
contrasts equal1 on that physical prefix, while the lower contrasts have zero
weighted mean there. Hence c_k=1+sum_{j>k}x_j is its mean density, and S_k is a
positive prefix mass times Cov(r,Phi'(r)). Abel summation has the stated signs.
For strictly convex Phi the full covariance is strictly positive whenever the
state is nonequilibrium, and the last ratio is positive. Finite-time exponential
injectivity excludes equilibrium for a distinct initial Gibbs preparation.
For nondifferentiable finite convex Phi, composition is locally Lipschitz on the
positive compact density range; the chain rule with ordered subgradients holds
almost everywhere. Strict convexity orders subgradients strictly at distinct
arguments, so strictness survives this extension and integration.

4. Constant rare-tail proposition in high_dimension_bound_reduction.tex

Weighted orthogonal compression is a conservative reversible physical generator
with the displayed weighted-average downward rates. A slow eigenfunction that is
constant on the tail belongs to the compressed subspace and remains an exact
eigenfunction. On the mean-zero compressed space its Rayleigh quotient cannot
exceed the full slow eigenvalue, and equality can only occur in the original
one-dimensional slow eigenspace. This proves both slow ordering and simplicity;
full dynamic lumpability is not needed.

Direct expansion of compressed F minus the weighted average of original F gives
exactly sum q_k(Q_T-q_k)G_ijk/(2Q_T q_j). All coefficients are nonnegative.
The aggregate condition Q_T<q3 ensures four distinct ordered compressed weights,
so the proved four-state arithmetic lemma applies. The first two contrasts have
the same coefficients before and after compression. The third compressed
coefficient is -(Q_T/Q3) times the constant tail value, hence nonnegative since
that value equals w_n<=0. Orientation is therefore consistent. The n/3 thermal
bound follows from the independently audited conditional proposition.

The proposition is read in the stated positive-rate reversible working domain.
For standalone reuse it would be clearer to repeat those hypotheses explicitly;
a zero-rate extension should not be inferred just from its abbreviated opening.
The constant-tail and aggregate-weight restrictions are substantive, and neither
this result nor the ratio obstruction resolves the unrestricted higher-n bound.
