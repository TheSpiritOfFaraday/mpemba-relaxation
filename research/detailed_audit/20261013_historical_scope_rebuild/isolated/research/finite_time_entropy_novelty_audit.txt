Checked 11 October 2026. This is a targeted comparison, not an exhaustive
priority search. No claim of first discovery is warranted by this audit.

Results compared
1. G-only, reversible finite-state Gibbs relaxation: the sufficient,

2. G-only plus tridiagonal weighted-Helmert contrast generator: all-time

Established tools, and what they do not alone prove
A. Binyamin Schwarz, Totally positive differential systems, Pacific J.
Math. 32 (1970), 203--229, Theorem 2 and introductory statement, pp.203--205.
Primary publisher text inspected:
https://msp.org/pjm/1970/32-1/pjm-v32-n1-p20-p.pdf
Schwarz proves that a continuous linear system has totally nonnegative
transition matrices exactly when its coefficient matrices are tridiagonal
with nonnegative off-diagonal entries. His older terminology calls this
"TP"; modern terminology is TN. He expressly credits Loewner for this
characterization. This subsumes our tridiagonal-exponential/compound-matrix
step entirely, including reducible nonnegative boundaries. It does not
state a Gibbs-preparation or entropy comparison. The retained application
requires the thermal derivative-ratio ordering and Helmert-prefix convex
covariance identity before Abel summation. Cite Schwarz (and, optionally,
Margaliot--Sontag's modern exposition, arXiv:1802.09590) for the standard
semigroup tool, not for the full thermal theorem.

B. Fill--Kahn, Comparison inequalities and fastest-mixing Markov chains,
arXiv:1109.6075v3 (local full text), Section 3, Proposition 3.2 and Corollary
3.3; Section 3.3 treats additional discrepancies through majorization.
https://arxiv.org/abs/1109.6075
Their comparison uses stochastically monotone reversible kernels and a
common initial distribution with monotone density ratio, comparing two
kernels. Our contrast-tridiagonal theorem compares Gibbs preparations
under one kernel and permits failure of physical-state stochastic
monotonicity (the exact three-state example in the fragment has negative
adjacent-difference K_21). Therefore the displayed theorem is not a direct
substitution into Proposition 3.2. The methods of positive-semigroup order,
density-ratio comparison, and convex-divergence ordering are established.

C. Ohga--Hayakawa--Ito, arXiv:2410.06623v2, previously inspected primary
full text (sources/2410.06623v2.txt), Result 2 and SI D2; SI B concerns the
KL counterpart of Result 1.
https://arxiv.org/html/2410.06623v2
The stochastic-monotonicity no-Mpemba framework is relevant precedent.
Our physical-state order theorem is an application of that established
framework and related Markov comparison methods. Neither new fragment
should be advertised as discovering monotonicity-based no-Mpemba arguments.
The contrast-tridiagonal example failing physical monotonicity explains
why the present hypotheses require a separate argument. No direct theorem
with either exact new hypothesis/conclusion pair was located in this text.

Other superficially close primary results checked
D. P. Ben-Abdallah, Mpemba Effect in Many-Body Systems Near Equilibrium,
arXiv:2603.11707v3, June 4 2026 version; primary HTML checked:
https://arxiv.org/html/2603.11707v3
Sections II--III study arbitrary small deviation vectors, linear relaxation
operators, and Euclidean global distances/componentwise ordering. This is
not a theorem for the one-parameter Gibbs curve, relative entropy, or a
rate-independent inverse-temperature window. Its positivity discussion is
an established ingredient shared with our argument, not a substitute for
the nonlinear KL Hessian bound. The HTML also prints an August 24 date;
the version header identifies v3 as June 4. No inference relies on resolving
that internal date discrepancy.

E. Alyuruk et al., Thermodynamic limits of the Mpemba effect: A unified
resource theory analysis, arXiv:2502.00123v2, primary HTML checked:
https://arxiv.org/html/2502.00123v2
Results 1--3 and the thermal-operation discussion compare correlated local
thermal preparations and possible conversion pathways using
thermomajorization. The reported temperature windows concern correlations
and available thermal operations. They do not give same-fixed-generator
KL derivative ordering for uncorrelated global Gibbs preparations under
the original G inequalities. Thus their use of "temperature window" should
be acknowledged as different in hypotheses and meaning, not treated as
establishing or contradicting our explicit sufficient constant.

Bounded search scope and limitations
Searches included Mpemba/relative entropy/total positivity; Mpemba/Helmert;
Mpemba/tridiagonal/entropy; Mpemba/near equilibrium/Markov/absence; and
temperature-window/resource-theory combinations. Original Schwarz text,
the two arXiv papers above, and existing local primary comparison texts
were inspected. Lu--Raz's PMC full-text endpoint returned a CAPTCHA during
this pass; no new claim about its exact linear-response statement is made.
A modern TP tutorial HTML endpoint failed, but the original Schwarz
publisher PDF was accessible. Search results and nonappearance of an exact
match do not establish novelty or a complete literature census.

Recommended manuscript characterization
"The following are problem-specific applications and quantitative
extensions of classical positivity, total-positivity, and convexity tools.
We give complete proofs; the bounded literature comparison did not locate
these exact statements, but we make no priority claim. The numerical
constant is a sufficient bound and is not asserted to be sharp."

The specific contribution that survives this comparison is the exact
Gibbs/Helmert-to-entropy bridge and, separately, its uniform quantitative
Hessian/angle estimate. TP2 propagation, positive-semigroup comparison,
convex covariance, and general near-equilibrium quadratic approximation
must remain identified as established tools. The full finite-time KL
heating question for unrestricted G-only generators remains unresolved;
neither these sources nor our restricted results settle it.
