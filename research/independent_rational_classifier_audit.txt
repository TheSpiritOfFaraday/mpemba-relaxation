SIGN AND MODEL CHECK
The code centers prime valuations, divides by four, and therefore computes
pi'(0), not its negative. Normalized bath weights define D and h. Using the
unnormalized supplied weights in the logarithms is valid because the centering
operator kills every common energy shift. The scalar evaluated at the largest
nonzero root equals p'(r)b(0)b'(0)/(w^T D w). Here p'(r)>0, and the full-premise
orientation has b(0)<0, so a positive scalar correctly means cooling.
The polynomial projector is used only on zero-sum d. The faster roots may
coincide; this does not invalidate the simple-slow projector formula.

ALGEBRAIC AND TRANSCENDENTAL CHECK
OrderedField uses the isolated CRootOf atom as its field generator, including
when the requested eigenvalue is a rational multiple of that atom. The
coefficient enclosure uses the same generator interval and Horner arithmetic.
It does not mistake the sign of an algebraic polynomial's leading coefficient
for the sign at the selected real embedding.
The logarithm routine reduces a prime to 2^e times a rational in [1,2), and
uses a valid positive atanh-series tail bound. All acceptance arithmetic is
rational or algebraic; floating computation appears only in independent audit
checks and in construction of deliberately difficult test inputs.

Baker's stated hypothesis was independently checked in the opening extract of
his 1967 primary paper, Linear forms in the logarithms of algebraic numbers (II):
https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/mathematika/article/abs/linear-forms-in-the-logarithms-of-algebraic-numbers-ii/123780AC339E1DAD5460D936F2C863CE
The extract supports precisely the implication used: rational independence of
the logarithms together with 2 pi i gives algebraic-coefficient independence
of the logarithms. Distinct positive-prime real logarithms meet the hypothesis
by unique factorization and separation of real and imaginary parts. Hence
coefficientwise zero is a valid exact equality test, and otherwise converging
intervals must eventually decide the nonzero scalar's sign. This is linear
independence, not algebraic independence. The DOI redirected to an inaccessible
Wiley page in this check; the publisher-hosted Cambridge extract was accessible.

LIMITS
This is an independent implementation and mathematical argument audit, not a
formal proof of SymPy's algebraic routines. It relies on the previously proved
full-triplet endpoint and degenerate-boundary theorems. The test inputs do not
establish performance bounds: integer factorization and near-zero forms can
be costly. General unconstrained generators, arbitrary real input, and transient
finite-time crossings remain outside the classifier's declared scope.
