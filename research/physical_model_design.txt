Study declared 12 October 2026 before numerical screening.

Four metastable configurations have ordered energies 0, g1, g1+g2,
g1+g2+g3. Basin A consists of configurations 0 and 3; basin B consists
of 1 and 2. This models interleaved energy levels in two conformational
basins, with a higher activation cost for changing basin.

Saddle energies are max(Ei,Ej)+h_A on edge (0,3),
max(Ei,Ej)+h_B on edge (1,2), and max(Ei,Ej)+h_X on all four
interbasin edges. A common attempt frequency is fixed to 1.
These are three shared activation parameters, chosen independently of
eigenvectors. The same bath generator acts on both thermal preparations.

Initial screening will use the previously declared benchmark box.
Hypothesis: rapid equilibration within each basin leaves a slow mismatch
of basin populations. Interleaved energies can make this mismatch
nonmonotonic in preparation temperature. A hotter preparation may then
have less mismatch while its larger intrabasin disturbance decays rapidly.

This is a phenomenological activated configuration network, not yet a
derived continuum potential or an experimental system. The barrier law,
metastability approximation, and microscopic realization must be assessed
separately. Known metastable-population mechanisms are not claimed novel.
All triplet tests will be evaluated, without assuming their failure.
Sampling is discovery, not a certified robust neighborhood.
