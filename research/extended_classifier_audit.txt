OUTCOME
No substantive mathematical or implementation defect was found in these five
files after independent derivations and additional exact boundary tests. This
is a source/code audit, not a formal verification of SymPy. The analytic
classification and exact commensurate implementation have different scopes;

1. UNIVERSAL SIMPLE-MODE TEST
The Wronskian identity has the stated sign: adjoining 1, multiplying all
columns by Z, and expanding W(Z,f_1,...,f_k) leaves precisely the terms
j>k with sign (-1)^(k(k-1)/2). In particular, u'<0 and the v(u) curvature
is strictly positive. The mixed Wronskians give the stated slope formula;
the slope increases strictly with x and ranges from -1 to s(1), with no
assumption that s(1) is positive. Thus Phi has at most one interior extremum
and its infimum/supremum on either side of the bath occur among the listed
values. Equality of -B/C with a limiting slope correctly creates no interior
extremum. C=0 and B=C=0 are handled.

The link to an ordinary effect uses the bath zero, not merely the existence
of a stationary point: absence of anomalous absolute-amplitude ordering
forces a fixed sign of b and a fixed weak sign of b' on each open side. In
the reverse direction a last/first sign-changing stationary point gives
the required interval of anomalous absolute-amplitude ordering. A stationary
bath does not invalidate this argument. Opposite strict endpoint signs persist
at finite temperatures; a zero endpoint alone is not treated as a finite
strong preparation. The strong-root knot procedure is complete because
between consecutive stationary knots the overlap is strictly monotone.

The Chebyshev bounds count multiplicity, and the interpolation/rank-one
construction attains them. The perturbation bound implies positive off-diagonal
rates; bath centering gives conservation. The realization does not imply the
triplet-failure premise, and the text explicitly avoids that implication.

2. DEGENERATE PROJECTIONS
The symmetric-projector form H=D^(-1/2) P D^(-1/2) is positive semidefinite,
annihilates the bath distribution, and gives the correct leading chi-squared
amplitude. The KL coefficient is half as large. Vanishing Q is equivalent
to cancellation of the entire slow eigenspace.

Distinct Gibbs vectors are independent up to the number of states by the
exponential evaluation determinant. Therefore the kernel-dimension argument
gives at most n-r-1 nonbath strong preparations. The proposed projector
construction realizes any prescribed set of that size while retaining
strictly positive rates and the desired slow multiplicity. The endpoint
r=n-1 remains valid: no prescribed extra preparation and a reset generator.

Independently expanded the formal polynomial R=(f^T H f)' Z-2(f^T H f)Z'
using f_i'=-E_i f_i and symmetric indeterminate H. All twelve squared-index
and four distinct-index coefficients match the text exactly; all pure cubes
cancel. Thus the 16-term/15-zero upper bound is valid, though not asserted
sharp. R cannot vanish identically for a nonzero slow projector. The finite
Rolle prescription properly disclaims a terminating oracle for arbitrary
transcendental equality problems.

Scope wording to tighten: the sentence saying strict ordering of Q
"classifies ... reversals" can be misread as a decision for every specified
pair. If Q(beta_f)=Q(beta_n), subleading terms can decide that pair's eventual
ordering. Strict inequality certifies the leading-amplitude reversal, and
the derivative test completely classifies EXISTENCE over preparation
temperatures. The finite-time chi-squared section already handles equal
leading coefficients correctly by discarding zero differences. This is a
wording caveat, not a defect in the existence classification.

3. EXACT COMMENSURATE TEST
With x=exp(-beta Delta), scalar derivative numerator is N'Z-NZ', while the
quadratic projector amplitude has numerator N'Z-2NZ'. Reversing the beta/x
orientation does not change whether there is an interior derivative sign
change. For a nonconstant scalar overlap vanishing at the bath, existence
of such a sign change on a side is equivalent to an ordinary effect there.
For nonnegative Q, the derivative's sign near the isolated bath zero fixes
the ordinary direction, so an interior odd-multiplicity root is likewise
necessary and sufficient. Even-multiplicity stationary roots alone do not
count. Square-free counts of N count distinct strong preparations, including
tangent zeros, while excluding the bath and the two temperature endpoints.

A repeated root of a rational cubic is rational: its irreducible minimal
polynomial would otherwise have degree at least two and occur twice,
requiring degree at least four. Thus the rank-two slow projector and the
rank-three reset projector are rational as claimed.

4. IMPLEMENTATION AUDIT
All declared checks use exact rational or algebraic-field operations.
The graph test permits precisely connected positive-rate support; reversibility
then gives irreducibility. The largest real nonzero root is the slow root.
Adjugate columns correctly return a left eigenvector through L=M^T, and
the code checks both its eigen equation and bath centering. In rank two,
P=L(L-fast I)/(alpha(alpha-fast)) is the backward slow projector, so
P D^(-1) equals the symmetric amplitude matrix H. Rank three is correct.

OrderedField now consistently uses the CRootOf atom as BOTH coefficient-field
generator and isolating-interval generator. This repairs the scaled-root
mismatch. Exact reduction detects zero before refinement. Horner interval
arithmetic encloses the reduced coefficient polynomial and converges to its
nonzero value, so refinement terminates. The root atom may appear with a
rational scale or affine offset without affecting this argument.

The Sturm implementation first square-frees, removes exact endpoint factors,
then computes sign variations at ordered rational endpoints using the chosen
real embedding. Removing even-multiplicity derivative factors before this
count is correct. Constant and identically zero root-count inputs are handled;
an identically zero thermal derivative is ruled out mathematically and
explicitly rejected. The implementation covers all three slow multiplicities.

6. FINITE-TIME TEST AND TRANSIENT EXAMPLE
Orthogonality yields exactly one exponential term per distinct nonzero
eigenvalue; equal-amplitude terms may be deleted. For three surviving terms,
the stationary equation gives exp((b-a)t)=-bC/(aB), so BC<0 and the ratio>1
are precisely the conditions for an interior positive time. Endpoints plus
that value determine the infimum. A strictly negative limit implies a finite
negative interval; a zero limit alone does not. At most two zeros counted
with multiplicity follows from the three-exponential Chebyshev property.

The initial chi-squared formula and logarithmic derivative are correct even
when 2 beta-beta_b is negative. Distinct same-side thermal preparations have
strict initial distance ordering. A positive eventual coefficient with a
negative interior minimum requires two crossings and at least three distinct
nonzero decay rates; hence at least four states. This claim is specific to
weighted Euclidean/chi-squared distance.

The transient construction's weighted projectors, transpose conversion,
positive-rate choice of rho, exact eigenpairs, and rational coefficient tests
are consistent. The two roots in (0,1) give finite times in the reverse order
under -log(x)/2, as reported. The text correctly does not imply the example
satisfies the triplet premise or lacks asymptotic anomalies for other pairs.
