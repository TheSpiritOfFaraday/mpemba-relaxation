CENTRAL RESULTS
1. The four-state ordinary-cooling triplet implication is false. A strictly
   positive reversible counterexample has a standalone exact rational checker.
2. Under the full four-state triplet-failure premise, the ordinary cooling
   slow-amplitude advantage has sharp supremum 25 percent. A controlled family
   approaches it with a uniform spectral gap and all triplet tests strict.
3. Within that premise, simple-mode cooling has a single-endpoint necessary
   and sufficient criterion. Repeated slow modes have an exact reset-plus-
   adjacent-pair classification and normal all-time convex-divergence ordering.
4. In any dimension, the weaker G constraints exclude strong effects and
   asymptotic heating. Nevertheless, full-triplet-failure examples approach
   amplitude advantages 1-3/n. A certified finite five-state example exceeds
   37.67 percent. These limits require large energy separation.
5. General four-state simple slow modes admit a finite asymptotic classification.
   Degenerate modes have a separate classification for the specified distances.
   Exact implementations cover general commensurate rational systems, and
   rational systems without commensurate energies under the full premise.
6. Finite-time results include a complete weighted-Euclidean test for a specified
   pair, a uniform relative-entropy ordering window around the bath, and normal
   all-temperature heating order for every convex divergence when the contrast
   generator is tridiagonal.

WHAT THE PERCENTAGES MEAN
The ordinary cooling advantage is 1-|b_hot|/|b_cold| for the asymptotic slow
amplitude. It is not a measured refrigeration gain or a universal percentage
reduction in relaxation time. Relative entropy has a quadratic leading
amplitude. The report treats finite-time distances separately.

WHAT IS STILL OPEN HERE
For n>4, the full-premise sharp amplitude supremum lies between 1-3/n and
1-2/n. A first-three slow-mode inequality would close the gap. It is proved
for four states and a constant rare-tail subfamily, but not in full generality.
An exact graph-coupling LP reduces proposed counterexamples to explicit
feasibility problems. Exact trace counterexamples rule out one proposed
shortcut; failed large numerical searches do not prove the missing inequality.

General finite-time relative-entropy and total-variation reversals are not
fully classified. The new entropy theorems cover specified hypotheses and
temperature windows, not every possible trajectory and distance.
